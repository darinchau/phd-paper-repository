# CLaMP 3: Universal Music Information Retrieval Across Unaligned Modalities and Unseen Languages

## One-line Summary

CLaMP 3 aligns sheet music (ABC), MIDI, audio (frozen MERT features), and multilingual text in one contrastive space using text as a bridge, trained on the 2.31M-pair M4-RAG corpus; it sets retrieval records for paired modalities but its emergent symbolic↔audio retrieval remains weak (MRR 0.03–0.06 against a 0.0075 random baseline).

## 2. Key Contributions

1. **A four-stage text-bridged alignment scheme.** Stage 1 aligns text with one music encoder; stage 2 freezes text and aligns a second music encoder; stage 3 unfreezes text to refine; stage 4 freezes text and re-aligns the first encoder to repair drift. This avoids requiring paired audio–symbolic data.
2. **M4-RAG.** 2.31 million music-text pairs generated by retrieval-augmented generation from titles/artists, with background, analysis, description, scene, tags, genre, region, language, and translations; 27 languages and 194 countries (Table 1, §3.2).
3. **WikiMT-X.** 1,000 triplets of sheet music, audio, and four text types (background, analysis, description, scene), the first benchmark aligning all three music modalities.
4. **Two model variants.** CLaMP 3_c2sa (symbolic-optimised, initialised from CLaMP 2) and CLaMP 3_saas (audio-optimised, symbolic→audio→audio→symbolic); reported SOTA on symbolic and audio retrieval plus multilingual generalisation.

## 3. Methodology and Architecture

- **Encoders.** Text: XLM-R-base (12 layers, 768 hidden). Symbolic: M3 (12 layers, 768 hidden), which consumes interleaved multi-track ABC and MTF-encoded MIDI as bar/message patches (512 patches or 32,768 characters). Audio: a 12-layer, 768-hidden transformer trained from scratch on frozen MERT-v1-95M features; each five-second clip is one averaged MERT embedding; up to 128 clips (640 s) per item.
- **Objective.** InfoNCE between text and music embeddings (Eq. 1), with in-batch negatives. All encoders project to a global average-pooled vector.
- **Training.** Symbolic alignment: up to 100 epochs, 8×H800, 4 days, lr 5e-5, batch 1,024. Audio alignment: 1 day, lr 1e-5, batch 2,048. M4-RAG split 99/1 train/validation.
- **Data construction.** Symbolic sources: WebMusicText (1.4M ABC) and MMD (1.5M MIDI), unified by converting each to the other format, yielding ~3M symbolic files. Audio: 160k hours from 1.8M online items, paired with generated text. Qwen2.5-72B writes annotations from top-10 web search results; a Boolean sufficiency flag and format/language filters remove weak entries.

## 4. Key Results and Benchmarks

**Paired retrieval (Table 2, p. 5, MRR).** Symbolic: CLaMP 3_c2sa 0.4498 on WikiMT (vs CLaMP 2 0.3438) and 0.2826 on MidiCaps (vs 0.2695). Audio: CLaMP 3_saas 0.1985 on SDD (vs TTMR++ 0.1612) and 0.1177 on MC-R (vs 0.0959). WikiMT-X sheet-music retrieval: background 0.4028, analysis 0.3382, description 0.0835, scene 0.1500. These are strong, consistent gains on the tasks the model was aligned for.

**Multilingual (Table 3, p. 5–6).** CLaMP 3_saas outperforms CLAP and TTMR++ on unseen languages; e.g. Finnish 0.1770 vs CLaMP 3_c2sa 0.1112. The evaluation uses machine-translated WikiMT-X text and reports BLEU back-translation scores of 36–63, so translation noise is a live confound; the authors acknowledge this.

**Emergent cross-modal retrieval (Table 4, p. 6, MRR).** This is the key negative result for audio–symbolic alignment:

| Direction | CLaMP 3_c2sa | CLaMP 3_saas |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sheet → performance (SP) | 0.4547 | 0.3262 |
| Sheet → audio (SA) | 0.0543 | 0.0578 |
| Performance → sheet (PS) | 0.5293 | 0.3146 |
| Performance → audio (PA) | 0.0313 | 0.0397 |
| Audio → sheet (AS) | 0.0492 | 0.0410 |
| Audio → performance (AP) | 0.0383 | 0.0303 |

Random MRR is 0.0075. Symbolic↔audio is therefore 4–8× random but an order of magnitude below symbolic↔symbolic. The authors call the alignment "relatively weak" and recommend paired data for supervised alignment.

**What this establishes.** Text can bridge unpaired modalities well enough for above-chance retrieval, and multilingual transfer works. It does not establish a usable audio–symbolic embedding for fine-grained matching, source identity, or mashup compatibility. The global pooled embedding also cannot represent temporal dynamics, which the authors name as limitation one.

## 5. Limitations and Future Work

- **No temporal dynamics.** One global vector per piece; the authors' Beethoven Fifth example shows motif-level development is missed. This matters for alignment, repair, and any note-level attribution.
- **Weak audio–symbolic alignment.** Emergent MRR 0.03–0.06; paired data or a stronger objective is required.
- **Translation-based multilingual evaluation.** No native multilingual benchmark; BLEU varies widely across languages.
- **Generated text is not ground truth.** M4-RAG annotations come from web retrieval plus an LLM; factual errors, hallucinated context, and title/artist ambiguity are filtered only by heuristics.
- **Computational cost.** Four days on 8 H800s for symbolic alignment, plus a large proprietary-scale audio corpus, is out of reach for a single-lab replication.
- **Copyright.** Audio is not redistributed; only 1.56M audio-text pairs are released.

## 7. Glossary

- **MIR:** Music Information Retrieval.
- **MRR:** Mean Reciprocal Rank.
- **InfoNCE:** Information Noise-Contrastive Estimation loss.
- **ABC:** a text notation for sheet music.
- **MIDI / MTF:** Musical Instrument Digital Interface / MIDI Text Format.
- **M4-RAG:** Million-scale Multilingual Music Metadata, the generated corpus.
- **RAG:** Retrieval-Augmented Generation.
- **SOTA:** State of the Art.

# 2026 Literature Map for Explainable Symbolic Music AI

This overview connects the 55 user-supplied papers processed in the October 2026 batch. Each paper page links here, and this page links back to every paper page. The set is intentionally exhaustive: adjacent or low-relevance papers remain visible and are labeled rather than silently omitted.

## Batch scope and evidence boundary

- **Denominator:** 55 supplied arXiv PDFs, all downloaded locally and read through full-paper digests; the canonical PDFs are in `papers/`.
- **Interpretation:** author claims, reported measurements, artifact checks, and repository inferences are not interchangeable. See each source record for page/section anchors.
- **Known integrity blocker:** the supplied `2609.28008v2.pdf` visually contains the EEG paper `2606.04040v2`, while its hidden text contains MIDIBACK. Its source record preserves the MIDIBACK text digest but flags the PDF mismatch; replace the PDF before relying on figures or visual page numbers.
- **Research focus:** the most relevant cluster is auditable symbolic generation/editing, source attribution, retrieval/recombination, symbolic representations, and verifiable theory constraints. Video/audio-only and non-music papers are retained as adjacent methods or negative evidence.

## Cross-paper synthesis

### Representation and structure

The batch moves from explicit symbolic event schemes (REMI, subword tokenization, MidiTok, POP909, MAESTRO) toward learned audio codecs and cross-modal latents. The strongest programme-relevant lesson is to keep a representation that can be inspected and edited separate from a renderer: learned semantic/audio tokens can improve generation but do not automatically expose notes, voices, source identity, or repair causes. Melody descriptors, hierarchical phrase/section analysis, chord embeddings, and group-invariant geometry offer complementary candidates for auditable features, but each needs construct-specific intervention tests.

### Generation, control, and editing

The control papers show a recurring pattern: add explicit controls, plans, retrieval candidates, or intermediate representations to a powerful generator. Music ControlNet, MotiGen, StepAudio 3 Music, incremental symbolic editing, MuseGAN, and the dance/video systems are useful interface precedents. For the current goal, the key distinction is whether the control is source-preserving and exact. Most audio systems preserve a condition statistically, not the original notes or provenance; RER, MIDIBACK, NoteSep, SpanSynth-Edit, and incremental editing are closer to executable repair/recombination. The performance-rendering papers add a complementary axis: Zeng et al. (2509.23878) make the style dimension an explicit, transferable, and score-recommendable factor, while Borovik et al. (2511.03425) show that flow-matching inpainting supports both rendering and region-level repair-like re-expression of a performance, with the caveat that its perceptual evidence rests on non-expert pairwise preference.

### Retrieval, recombination, and mashups

RER explicitly separates retrieval, music-theoretic editing, and reranking, making the decision trace inspectable. POP909 and hierarchical structure analysis support aligned material and section-aware controls. SpanSynth-Edit and NoteSep suggest local edit regions and score-informed ownership, but audio similarity is not proof of exact source preservation. The immediate gap is a candidate-conditioned symbolic recomposer whose trace cites source events, conflict locations, protected material, alternatives, and counterfactual changes.

### Explainability, attribution, and verification

Open-Qwen-Music treats provenance and component diagnostics as first-class artifacts; TrueMuse and the attribution/plagiarism papers make data/source attribution measurable; MusicRLVR turns symbolic constraints into deterministic verifiers; group-invariant statistics and tonal-embedding papers connect representation geometry to interpretable symmetries. These precedents support a layered audit trail, but probing or a readable trace alone is not a faithful explanation. Faithfulness requires interventions, counterfactuals, and output changes attributable to the cited evidence. DC-SAE (Chen et al. 2026) is the batch's clearest instance of that ladder taken seriously on a music generator: probing establishes recoverable work-identity/performance-variation factors, and a single-feature steering intervention — with its selection-scoring circularity acknowledged — supplies the (small-effect) causal step, while the utility step for musicians remains untested.

### Evaluation and human interaction

Rubric-based optimization, MIRA, Co-policy, Encypher, Reimagining Dance, and MusicAgent emphasize intent, interaction, or evaluation. Their risks are evaluator bias, closed APIs, confounded interfaces, and descriptive user studies. The current research should keep symbolic metrics, source identity, listener judgements, and explanation utility as separate outcomes rather than a single weighted score. MATCHA (Batlle-Roca et al. 2026) supplies the missing perceptual axis for exactly this separation: attribute-level expert judgments agree substantially (melody κ ≈ 0.60) where global-similarity agreement historically collapses, and its Kendall-τ audit shows each audio similarity metric tracks only its own attribute slice — CoverID melody/harmony, CLAP/DEfNet rhythm/voice/timbre, KL/PaSST neither tonal dimension — so any claim that one score measures "musical similarity" now has a human-grounded refutation, while symbolic-domain detection remains unbenchmarked. The cross-domain caution comes from ArtiAgent (Park et al. 2026), the newest vision-side detect→explain→repair pipeline: its correction loop is verified by the same VLM that guided the repair, its repair results are qualitative only, and its artifact-free generation is scored by a reward model trained on its own synthetic pairs — a concrete instance of the self-metric circularity this separation rule exists to prevent, and the closest published baseline against which the SLOP repair-evaluation protocol must position itself.

### Adjacent and low-relevance methods

LapSRN, RCAN, CDDFuse, EEG reconstruction, pronunciation geometry, Alzheimer screening, and the Pain Axis are not direct music methods. They remain useful as transfer precedents for progressive reconstruction, channel-preserving representations, native-reference geometry, cross-corpus evidence anchoring, and intervention-based interpretability; none should be cited as direct evidence for symbolic music quality. NCM (Kumbong et al. 2026) joins this group as a text-domain resolution ladder: its level schedule is a measured design lever (exponential and quadratic ladders roughly halve Gen. PPL against a linear one at equal budget), and its coarse-code fixing, mixing, and perturbation probes are the closest text-side analogue of a level intervention, but the probes are single-example and the paper contains no music task, so it supports hierarchical scheduling and probe design rather than any symbolic-music claim. ArtiAgent (Park et al. 2026) also lives here as a vision-domain method, but at higher priority than the rest of this group: it is the closest published artifact-repair pipeline to the SLOP side line's substrate, its annotator-free paired-data construction (perception → injection → curation) is a transferable recipe for building repair-training corpora, and its released-artifact scope (synthesis pipeline + ArtiBench only; no checkpoints, no 100K set, no correction code) is verified in the SLOP 4b handoff.

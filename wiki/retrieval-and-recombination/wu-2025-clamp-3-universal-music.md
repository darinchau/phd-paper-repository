# CLaMP 3: Universal Music Information Retrieval Across Unaligned Modalities and Unseen Languages

## Summary

CLaMP 3 puts sheet music, MIDI, audio, and multilingual text into one contrastive embedding space without requiring paired audio–symbolic data: text is aligned first to one music encoder, then to the other, with freeze/unfreeze stages that limit drift. It is trained on M4-RAG (2.31M generated music-text pairs) and evaluated on WikiMT-X (1,000 sheet/audio/text triplets). Paired retrieval improves substantially over CLaMP 2 and CLAP/TTMR++. The important caveat for this programme is the emergent symbol↔audio result: MRR 0.03–0.06 versus 0.0075 random, an order of magnitude below symbol↔symbol. Text bridging gives above-chance alignment, not a usable fine-grained cross-modal space. The authors also identify the global-pooling limitation: one vector per piece cannot represent temporal development.

## Key Contributions

- A four-stage text-bridged contrastive recipe that aligns unpaired modalities and preserves earlier alignment.
- M4-RAG: 2.31M music-text pairs with long-form background/analysis/description/scene fields, 27 languages, 194 countries.
- WikiMT-X: the first text–sheet–audio triplet benchmark, with four semantic text types.
- Two specialised variants (symbolic-optimised c2sa, audio-optimised saas) with SOTA paired retrieval and cross-lingual generalisation to unseen languages.

## Methodology and Architecture

Text encoder XLM-R-base; symbolic encoder M3 over bar-level ABC or MTF MIDI patches; audio encoder a 12-layer transformer over frozen MERT-v1-95M clip embeddings (5 s per clip, up to 128 clips). InfoNCE with in-batch negatives aligns each music encoder to text. Stage order for c2sa: text+symbolic, then frozen text+audio. For saas: symbolic→audio→audio→symbolic. Training used 8×H800 (4 days symbolic, 1 day audio). M4-RAG annotations are generated by Qwen2.5-72B from top-10 web results, filtered by a sufficiency flag, format checks, language detection, and post-processing; a random language is chosen per item for translation.

Paired retrieval is the strong result; emergent audio–symbolic retrieval is the negative result. Multilingual gains hold on unseen languages but are evaluated on machine translations. Global mean pooling means the embedding cannot localise a motif, a bar, or a conflicting note.
